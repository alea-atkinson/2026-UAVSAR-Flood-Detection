## Is tile_46_48 a serious failure or mostly low-confidence threshold movement?

`tile_46_48.tif` had the highest flip percentage in the full 138-tile run (3.2181% of pixels, 2109 pixels). Its mean distance-to-threshold among flipped pixels is **0.0148** (P95 = 0.0257), and 100.0% of its flipped pixels sit within 0.1 of the 0.5 threshold. Of its flipped pixels, 6.07% MOVED FROM WRONG TO RIGHT (improved) and 93.93% moved from RIGHT TO WRONG (degraded), for a small net Dice change of -0.00424. **Conclusion: this is mostly low-confidence threshold movement on pixels the float model itself was already uncertain about, not a serious hardware-style quantization failure** -- see `diagnostic_tile_46_48.png` for the spatial pattern.

## Why can tile_42_44 have a large Dice diff despite only 0.3555% changed pixels?

`tile_42_44.tif` had the largest ABSOLUTE Dice change in the full 138-tile run (+0.03808) despite only 0.3555% of pixels flipping (233 pixels). This is explained by pixel-level class imbalance combined with Dice's own formula, not by the flipped pixels being unusually confident: Dice = 2*TP / (2*TP + FP + FN). When the ground-truth flood region in a tile is small (a small denominator), even a handful of flipped pixels landing as new true positives (fixing false negatives) can move Dice by a large relative amount, purely because the denominator itself is small -- of this tile's flipped pixels, 58.80% improved (wrong -> right) versus 41.20% degraded (right -> wrong), a net improvement that Dice's small-denominator sensitivity then amplifies into a large-looking Dice diff. Mean distance-to-threshold among its flipped pixels is 0.0058, consistent with the same low-confidence-boundary pattern seen in the other tiles, not a large jump in a confidently-decided pixel.

## Limitations

- **Diagnostic of a small, explicitly-named tile set** (4
  tiles), chosen because they were outliers in the full 138-tile fp2 run --
  not a claim about the full split's flip-location distribution in general,
  though the pooled pattern here is consistent with the earlier full-split
  aggregate finding (0.158% pixels changed, tiny Dice/IoU impact).
- **First-layer substitution only**, exactly as in
  `analyze_first_layer_fixed_point_segmentation_impact.py`: every layer
  except `enc1.block.0` -> `enc1.block.1` -> ReLU runs the original
  trained float PyTorch weights unmodified.
- **This is NOT a full FPGA U-Net and NOT VHDL simulation.**
- **Quantization scheme is a Python-only approximation** of a plausible
  hardware datapath, not a scheme implemented or verified in VHDL for
  real (non-toy) input.
- **No board testing, no measured hardware speedup, no measured hardware
  power** are claimed or estimated anywhere in this script.
