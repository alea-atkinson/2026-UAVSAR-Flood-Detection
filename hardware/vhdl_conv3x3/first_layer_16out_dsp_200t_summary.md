# 16-Output DSP-Aware First-Layer Convolution on Artix-7 200T

Date: July 8, 2026

This document covers a new hardware prototype and its synthesis results
only. No board testing, no measured speedup, and no measured board power
are claimed anywhere in this document.

## 1. Purpose

`hardware/vhdl_conv3x3/larger_fpga_target_comparison_summary.md` showed
that the existing, verified 8-output DSP-aware first-layer design
(`stream_conv3x3_3chan_8out_cell_dsp`) behaves very differently on the two
targets already used in this project:

| Target | LUTs | DSPs | Warnings |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Artix-7 35T (`xc7a35tcpg236-1`) | 6991 (33.61%) | 90/90 (100%, overutilized) | 1 (DSP overutilization) |
| Artix-7 200T (`xc7a200tsbg484-1`) | 668 (0.50%) | 203/740 (27.43%) | 0 |

This document tests whether that same headroom lets a **more parallel**
DSP-aware first-layer design -- 16 output channels instead of 8 -- also
synthesize cleanly on the 200T, as a real synthesized midpoint between the
verified 8-output design and the purely estimated 32-output scaling
projection in `first_layer_32out_scaling_estimate.md`.

## 2. Design scope

- First-layer convolution only: one `Conv2d(3, base_channels, 3)` layer's
  output channels, computed directly from real trained-model INT8 weights.
- **16 of 32 output channels** (kernels 0-15 of `enc1.block.0.weight`).
- No BatchNorm folding, no bias beyond the (zero) Conv2d bias, no ReLU, no
  padding, no downstream U-Net layers.
- No board testing, no measured speedup, no measured board power, no
  deployment claim.
- Primary synthesis target: Artix-7 200T, `xc7a200tsbg484-1`.
- Optional comparison target: Artix-7 35T, `xc7a35tcpg236-1` (also run, to
  show the same DSP-overutilization pattern the 8-output design showed).

## 3. How the 16-output design was built from the 8-output DSP-aware design

`stream_conv3x3_3chan_16out_cell_dsp.vhd` and
`tb_stream_conv3x3_3chan_16out_cell_dsp.vhd` are **new files** -- the
existing, verified `stream_conv3x3_3chan_8out_cell_dsp.vhd` and its
testbench were not modified. The 16-output design is a mechanical
extension of the 8-output DSP-aware design, generated from the same
structural pattern:

- The same 3 `window3x3_stream` instances (one per input channel), shared
  by all output kernels -- unchanged from the 8-output design.
- The same `conv3x3_dot_pipelined_dsp` module (DSP-steered multiplies),
  instantiated 16 x 3 = 48 times instead of 8 x 3 = 24 times (one triplet
  of channel dot-products per kernel).
- The same single shared `valid_dot` gate (kernel 0 / channel 0's
  dot-product `valid_out`) driving all final summation registers.
- The same 4-cycle latency, same 100 MHz clock target, same zero-bias
  Conv2d datapath.
- Weight/golden-output source: **kernels 0-15 of `enc1.block.0.weight`**,
  generated by re-running the existing
  `scripts/export_first_layer_multi_kernel_vhdl_vectors.py` script (already
  generalized to accept any channel range) with argument `0-15`, producing
  `hardware/vhdl_conv3x3/first_layer_kernels0_to15_pkg.vhd` and matching
  JSON/CSV/Markdown test-vector artifacts under
  `hardware/vhdl_conv3x3/test_vectors/multi_kernel_first_layer/`. No new
  test-vector generation script was written -- the existing script already
  supported an arbitrary channel range.
- Kernels 0-7's weights and expected outputs in the new package are
  bit-identical to the existing `first_layer_kernels0_to7_pkg.vhd` (same
  checkpoint, same quantization method, same toy input), confirmed by
  comparing GHDL PASS output values between the two testbenches.

This is not a new convolution architecture: it is the same
window-sharing / DSP-steered-multiply structure used throughout this
project's first-layer prototypes, extended to a different (larger) kernel
count.

## 4. Kernels used

**Kernels 0 through 15 of `enc1.block.0.weight`** (16 of the 32 total
first-layer output channels), from
`models/alea_tuned_filtered_strict_fp2_focaldice_adamw_20epochs_best.pt`.
Confirmed via `first_layer_kernels0_to15_pkg.vhd`'s `KERNEL_IDS` constant

## 8. Comparison to 8-output DSP-aware on 35T and 200T

Optional 35T run for this same 16-output design was also performed
(`synth_stream_conv3x3_3chan_16out_cell_dsp_35t.tcl`), for direct
comparison:

| Design | Target | LUTs (util%) | Regs (util%) | DSPs (util%) | WNS (ns) | WHS (ns) | Total power (W) | Warnings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 8-out DSP-aware | 35T | 6991 (33.61%) | 4405 (10.59%) | 90/90 (100%, overutilized) | +2.371 | +0.262 | 0.346 | 1 (DSP overutilized: used=266, avail=90) |
| 8-out DSP-aware | 200T | 668 (0.50%) | 410 (0.15%) | 203/740 (27.43%) | +3.848 | +0.262 | 0.373 | 0 |
| **16-out DSP-aware** | **35T** | **16789 (80.72%)** | **11004 (26.45%)** | **90/90 (100%, overutilized)** | **+2.355** | **+0.262** | **0.573** | **1 (DSP overutilized: used=529, avail=90)** |
| **16-out DSP-aware** | **200T** | **929 (0.69%)** | **418 (0.16%)** | **405/740 (54.73%)** | **+3.848** | **+0.262** | **0.617** | **0** |

Observations:

- On the 35T, doubling the kernel count from 8 to 16 makes the DSP
  overutilization **worse**, not just present: the requested-multiply
  count roughly doubled (266 -> 529, consistent with roughly doubling the
  16-kernel-vs-8-kernel multiply count), while the still-clamped 90/90
  DSPs force far more LUT fallback -- LUT utilization jumps from 33.61% to
  **80.72%**, more than double, because a larger share of the un-mapped
  multiplies has to fall back to LUT fabric with only the same 90 fixed
  DSPs available.
- On the 200T, doubling the kernel count leaves the design comfortably
  within budget: LUTs go from 668 (0.50%) to 929 (0.69%) -- a modest
  increase, not a doubling, since the 3 shared window generators and other
  fixed overhead do not scale with kernel count. DSP usage roughly doubles
  as expected (203 -> 405), reaching 54.73% of the 200T's 740 DSPs, still
  with **zero warnings**.
- Register counts stay nearly flat between the 8-output and 16-output
  DSP-aware designs on the 200T (410 -> 418), unlike the LUT-only parallel
  designs in the earlier larger-target comparison, where register counts
  scaled roughly with kernel count. This is consistent with
  `conv3x3_dot_pipelined_dsp`'s internal pipeline registers being absorbed
  into the DSP48E1 hard macros' own internal register stages once enough
  DSP resources are available to avoid LUT fallback, rather than being
  implemented as separate Slice Registers -- this is offered as the most
  likely explanation, not a confirmed root-cause analysis.
- WNS is identical between the 8-output and 16-output 200T runs (+3.848
  ns) and nearly identical on the 35T (+2.371 ns vs +2.355 ns), consistent
  with the critical path running through the same per-kernel dot-product
  pipeline structure regardless of how many kernels are instantiated in
  parallel.

### Relevance to the 32-output DSP demand estimate

`first_layer_32out_scaling_estimate.md` projected roughly 864 multiplies
for a full 32-kernel DSP-only mapping -- more than the 200T's 740 DSPs.
This 16-output measurement gives a real synthesized data point partway
there: 432 raw multiplies (16 kernels x 3 channels x 9 taps) produced a
DSP overutilization *request* of 529 on synthesis, of which **405 DSPs
were actually used** when 740 were available (200T). Scaling that request
figure by 2x for 32 kernels would suggest a requested-multiply count near
1058, further above the 200T's 740-DSP budget than the simpler 864-tap
estimate suggested -- **this makes the previous 32-output DSP-only
estimate look, if anything, optimistic relative to what synthesis actually
requests**, not more achievable. This is still an extrapolation from a
16-kernel data point, not a 32-kernel synthesis result.

## 9. Interpretation

- The 16-output DSP-aware first-layer design **passes GHDL** on the toy
  input (144/144 checks) and the existing 8-output DSP-aware regression
  still passes, confirming no numerical regression from the extension.
- The 16-output DSP-aware design **synthesizes cleanly on Artix-7 200T**
  (0 warnings, timing met, 54.73% DSP utilization, well under 1% LUT
  utilization).
- On the Artix-7 35T, the same 16-output design **does not** synthesize
  cleanly -- it inherits and worsens the DSP-overutilization problem seen
  in the 8-output design, with LUT utilization more than doubling to
  80.72% as a direct consequence.
- This provides a real synthesized midpoint between the verified 8-output
  design and the purely estimated 32-output design: it confirms the
  larger target's DSP headroom scales to at least 16 parallel DSP-aware
  output channels without warnings, while also showing (via the 35T
  comparison and the requested-multiply scaling above) that the small
  device's DSP ceiling gets proportionally *worse*, not better, as
  parallelism increases.
- This result **helps test whether the larger target makes more parallel
  first-layer DSP-aware scaling feasible**: for 16 channels, yes, on this
  specific larger target; for the full 32-channel layer, the
  requested-multiply scaling trend argues the 200T's 740 DSPs would still
  likely be insufficient for a fully DSP-only mapping, though a mixed
  DSP+LUT mapping (which Vivado already does automatically when DSPs are
  scarce) was not measured at 32 channels and might still fit with
  modest LUT usage, extrapolating from how little LUT growth occurred
  between 8 and 16 kernels on the 200T.

## 10. Limitations

- **First-layer convolution only.** No other layer, block, or model stage
  was implemented or synthesized.
- **16 output channels only, not all 32.** The full first Conv2d layer
  has `base_channels` (32 in the tuned model) output channels; this
  prototype covers channels 0-15 only.
- **No BatchNorm, folded bias, ReLU, or padding in VHDL.** The Conv2d
  bias used is 0 for every kernel (the layer's real bias is absorbed into
  a downstream BatchNorm that is not folded into this datapath).
- **No downstream U-Net layers.** This is not encoder/decoder inference,
  not a full U-Net, and not flood-segmentation inference.
- **No board testing.** All results are Vivado out-of-context synthesis
  results only.
- **No measured speedup.** No throughput or latency was measured on
  hardware; the 4-cycle pipeline latency is a simulated/synthesized
  cycle-accurate property, not a measured one.
- **No measured board power.** All power figures are Vivado's
  vector-less, "Medium confidence" `report_power` estimates, not
  measurements from a running device.
- **Vivado power is a tool estimate**, for both the 200T and the optional
  35T comparison run.
- **200T synthesis is a target comparison, not physical deployment.** The
  `xc7a200tsbg484-1` part was chosen as a larger, available Vivado
  synthesis target (see `larger_fpga_target_comparison_summary.md` for
  the selection rationale); no board of this or any part is claimed to be
  owned, tested, or deployed to.
- The requested-DSP-multiply figures reported by Vivado's overutilization
  warning (266 for 8 kernels, 529 for 16 kernels) are **larger** than the
  raw architectural multiply count (216 and 432, respectively); the exact
  cause of this discrepancy was not investigated further in this task and
  should not be assumed to be a simple 1:1 count of `w0..w8 x pixel`
  multiplies.

## 11. Recommended next step

Given that the 200T synthesized the 16-output DSP-aware design cleanly at
54.73% DSP utilization but the requested-multiply scaling trend suggests
32 channels would still exceed the 200T's DSP budget under a pure
DSP-only mapping, the most informative next experiment would be to build
and synthesize a 24-output DSP-aware design (roughly halfway between 16
and 32) on the 200T. That would give a third real synthesized data point
to fit a trend line against the DSP-overutilization request numbers
already measured at 8 and 16 outputs (266 and 529), rather than
extrapolating directly from 16 to 32 output channels in one step.
