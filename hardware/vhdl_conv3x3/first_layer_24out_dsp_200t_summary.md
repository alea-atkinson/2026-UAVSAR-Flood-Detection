# 24-Output DSP-Aware First-Layer Convolution on Artix-7 200T

Date: July 8, 2026

This document covers a new hardware prototype and its synthesis results
only. No board testing, no measured speedup, and no measured board power
are claimed anywhere in this document.

## 1. Purpose

Real DSP-aware synthesis data points already exist for 8 and 16 output
channels on the Artix-7 200T
(`hardware/vhdl_conv3x3/first_layer_16out_dsp_200t_summary.md`):

| Design | LUTs | Registers | DSPs | WNS | Warnings |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 8-output DSP-aware, 200T | 668 | 410 | 203/740 | +3.848 ns | 0 |
| 16-output DSP-aware, 200T | 929 | 418 | 405/740 | +3.848 ns | 0 |

Both prior points synthesized with **zero warnings** and comfortable DSP
headroom. This document builds and synthesizes a **24-output** DSP-aware
design -- the next real scaling point between the verified 16-output
design and the full 32-channel first layer -- to test whether that
headroom continues, or where it runs out.

## 2. Design scope

- First-layer convolution only: one `Conv2d(3, base_channels, 3)` layer's
  output channels, computed directly from real trained-model INT8 weights.
- **24 of 32 output channels** (kernels 0-23 of `enc1.block.0.weight`).
- No BatchNorm folding, no bias beyond the (zero) Conv2d bias, no ReLU, no
  padding, no downstream U-Net layers, no full model pipeline.
- No board testing, no measured speedup, no measured board power, no
  deployment claim.
- Primary synthesis target: Artix-7 200T, `xc7a200tsbg484-1`.
- Optional comparison target: Artix-7 35T, `xc7a35tcpg236-1` (also run).

## 3. How the 24-output design was built from the 16-output DSP-aware design

`stream_conv3x3_3chan_24out_cell_dsp.vhd` and
`tb_stream_conv3x3_3chan_24out_cell_dsp.vhd` are **new files** -- the
existing, verified `stream_conv3x3_3chan_16out_cell_dsp.vhd` and
`stream_conv3x3_3chan_8out_cell_dsp.vhd` (and their testbenches) were not
modified. The 24-output design is a mechanical extension of the same
structural pattern used by the 8- and 16-output DSP-aware designs:

- The same 3 `window3x3_stream` instances (one per input channel), shared
  by all output kernels.
- The same `conv3x3_dot_pipelined_dsp` module (DSP-steered multiplies),
  instantiated 24 x 3 = 72 times (one triplet of channel dot-products per
  kernel), vs. 48 for the 16-output design and 24 for the 8-output design.
- The same single shared `valid_dot` gate (kernel 0 / channel 0's
  dot-product `valid_out`) driving all final summation registers.
- The same 4-cycle latency, same 100 MHz clock target, same zero-bias
  Conv2d datapath.
- Weight/golden-output source: **kernels 0-23 of `enc1.block.0.weight`**,
  generated by re-running the existing, already-generalized
  `scripts/export_first_layer_multi_kernel_vhdl_vectors.py` script with
  argument `0-23` -- no new export script was needed. This produced
  `hardware/vhdl_conv3x3/first_layer_kernels0_to23_pkg.vhd` and matching
  JSON/CSV/Markdown test-vector artifacts under
  `hardware/vhdl_conv3x3/test_vectors/multi_kernel_first_layer/`.
- Kernels 0-15's INT8 weight constants in the new
  `first_layer_kernels0_to23_pkg.vhd` package were spot-checked
  (`grep`-compared, kernels 0, 5, 10, 15) against
  `first_layer_kernels0_to15_pkg.vhd` and found **bit-identical**.

This is not a new convolution architecture: it is the same
window-sharing / DSP-steered-multiply structure used throughout this
project's first-layer prototypes, extended to a different (larger) kernel
count.

## 4. Kernels used

**Kernels 0 through 23 of `enc1.block.0.weight`** (24 of the 32 total
first-layer output channels), from
`models/alea_tuned_filtered_strict_fp2_focaldice_adamw_20epochs_best.pt`.
Confirmed via `first_layer_kernels0_to23_pkg.vhd`'s `KERNEL_IDS` constant
`(0, 1, ..., 23)` and `NUM_KERNELS := 24`.

## 7. 200T synthesis results (primary target)

`stream_conv3x3_3chan_24out_cell_dsp` on `xc7a200tsbg484-1`
(`synth_stream_conv3x3_3chan_24out_cell_dsp_200t.tcl`):

| Resource | Used | Available | Util% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LUTs | 3374 | 134,600 | 2.51% |
| Registers | 706 | 269,200 | 0.26% |
| DSP48E1 | 731 | 740 | 98.78% |
| Block RAM tiles | 0 | 365 | 0.00% |

- WNS (setup slack): **+2.760 ns** (100 MHz / 10.000 ns clock still met).
- WHS (hold slack): **+0.262 ns**.
- Total on-chip power (estimate): **1.041 W** (Dynamic 0.914 W, Static
  0.127 W).
- **Synthesis warnings: 1** -- `WARNING: [Synth 8-3323] Resources of type
  DSP have been overutilized. Used = 788, Available = 740.` `synth_design`
  still completed successfully; Vivado's synthesis-time resource-sharing
  fell back enough multiplies to LUT fabric that the **final mapped** DSP
  count (731) settled just under the 740-DSP budget, at the cost of the
  LUT count jumping from 929 (16-output) to 3374 -- more than 3.6x for
  only 1.5x more kernels, consistent with a disproportionate share of the
  additional 8 kernels' multiplies being pushed to LUT fabric rather than
  cleanly mapped to spare DSP48E1 slices.

**The 24-output DSP-aware design synthesizes on the Artix-7 200T and meets
timing, but is no longer comfortably within budget: it triggers a DSP
overutilization warning during synthesis and finishes at 98.78% DSP
utilization, a sharp change from the 8- and 16-output designs' 0-warning,
comfortable-headroom results.**

## 8. Optional 35T synthesis results

`stream_conv3x3_3chan_24out_cell_dsp` on `xc7a35tcpg236-1`
(`synth_stream_conv3x3_3chan_24out_cell_dsp_35t.tcl`), run as an optional
comparison point:

| Resource | Used | Available | Util% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LUTs | 26,535 | 20,800 | **127.57%** |
| Registers | 17,407 | 41,600 | 41.84% |
| DSP48E1 | 91 | 90 | **101.11%** |
| Block RAM tiles | 0 | 50 | 0.00% |

- WNS: +4.765 ns. WHS: +0.262 ns. Total power: 0.804 W (Dynamic 0.734 W,
  Static 0.070 W).
- **Synthesis warnings: 1** -- `DSP have been overutilized. Used = 788,
  Available = 90.`
- **On the 35T, both LUT utilization (127.57%) and DSP utilization
  (101.11%) exceed 100%.** `synth_design` (netlist generation) still
  reports success because out-of-context synthesis does not perform
  placement -- but a utilization figure above 100% means this design
  **would not fit** if placement/implementation were attempted on this
  specific part. This is a materially different (and more severe) result
  than the 8- and 16-output designs' 35T runs, which stayed at or near
  100% DSP utilization but never exceeded 100% LUT utilization (33.61% and
  80.72% respectively).

## 9. Comparison to 8-output and 16-output DSP-aware results

| Design | Target | LUT util% | Reg util% | DSP util% | WNS (ns) | Total power (W) | Warnings |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 8-out | 35T | 33.61% | 10.59% | 100.00% (req=266) | +2.371 | 0.346 | 1 |
| 8-out | 200T | 0.50% | 0.15% | 27.43% | +3.848 | 0.373 | 0 |
| 16-out | 35T | 80.72% | 26.45% | 100.00% (req=529) | +2.355 | 0.573 | 1 |
| 16-out | 200T | 0.69% | 0.16% | 54.73% | +3.848 | 0.617 | 0 |
| **24-out** | **35T** | **127.57%** | **41.84%** | **101.11% (req=788)** | **+4.765** | **0.804** | **1** |
| **24-out** | **200T** | **2.51%** | **0.26%** | **98.78% (req=788)** | **+2.760** | **1.041** | **1** |

Trend in the DSP overutilization *request* figure (the "Used = N" number
in Vivado's warning, before synthesis-time fallback), which is target
independent since it reflects the design's raw multiply demand before any
fallback decision:

| Output channels | Requested DSPs (Vivado warning) |
|---:|---:|
| 8 | 266 |
| 16 | 529 |
| 24 | 788 |

This is very close to linear (roughly +263 per +8 output channels), which
is expected since each added kernel contributes a fixed 3 channels x 9
taps = 27 raw multiplies, plus a small constant overhead. Extrapolating
this trend to 32 outputs would put the request figure near **1050-1060**,
consistent with (and slightly above) the "roughly 864 multiplies"
estimate in `first_layer_32out_scaling_estimate.md` -- both figures point
in the same direction: **a 32-output DSP-only mapping would substantially
exceed the 200T's 740-DSP budget**, more so than the 24-output result
already does.

Key change at 24 outputs, on both targets:

- **200T**: still synthesizes and meets timing, but crosses from
  "comfortable headroom, 0 warnings" (8- and 16-output) to "at the edge of
  the DSP budget, 1 warning, large LUT-fallback cost" (24-output). DSP
  utilization jumped from 54.73% (16-out) to 98.78% (24-out) -- only 9
  DSPs of headroom remain.
- **35T**: crosses from "DSP-constrained but LUT count still under 100%"
  (8- and 16-output, both under the 20,800-LUT ceiling) to **"exceeds both
  DSP and LUT budgets"** (24-output, 127.57% LUT, 101.11% DSP) -- this
  design would not fit on the 35T even after LUT fallback.

## 10. Interpretation for scaling toward 32 output channels

- The 24-output DSP-aware first-layer design **passes GHDL** on the toy
  input (216/216 checks) and both prior regressions (8-output, 16-output)
  still pass, confirming no numerical regression from the extension.
- **The 24-output DSP-aware design synthesizes on Artix-7 200T**, meeting
  timing (+2.760 ns), but with a DSP overutilization warning and only
  9 DSPs of headroom remaining (731/740 used, 98.78%).
- This provides a **third real synthesized scaling point** after 8 and 16
  outputs, and it changes the picture from the earlier 16-output result:
  the 200T's DSP headroom is not open-ended -- it runs out between 16 and
  24 output channels, not between 24 and 32 as might have been hoped.
- Extrapolating the now-3-point request trend (266, 529, 788) to 32
  channels puts the request figure meaningfully above the 200T's 740-DSP
  budget, reinforcing (not overturning) the earlier
  `first_layer_32out_scaling_estimate.md` conclusion that a **pure
  DSP-only mapping for all 32 first-layer channels is unlikely to fit** on
  this target.
- **Whether 32-output DSP-aware synthesis is worth attempting next on the
  200T**: given that 24 outputs already overutilizes the DSP budget (by a
  modest 48-DSP margin, 788 requested vs. 740 available) and Vivado's
  fallback strategy already produces a large, disproportionate LUT-fallback
  cost at 24 outputs (3374 LUTs, up from 929 at 16 outputs), a full
  32-output attempt would very likely reproduce the same
  DSP-overutilization warning pattern with an even larger LUT-fallback
  cost, similar in character (though probably less severe, since the 200T
  has far more LUT headroom than the 35T) to what the 35T already shows at
  24 outputs. It is a reasonable next experiment, but it should be
  expected to overutilize DSPs and rely heavily on LUT fallback rather
  than to reproduce the clean, 0-warning results seen at 8 and 16 outputs.

## 11. Limitations

- **First-layer convolution only.** No other layer, block, or model stage
  was implemented or synthesized.
- **24 output channels only, not all 32.** The full first Conv2d layer
  has `base_channels` (32 in the tuned model) output channels; this
  prototype covers channels 0-23 only.
- **No BatchNorm, folded bias, ReLU, or padding in VHDL.** The Conv2d
  bias used is 0 for every kernel (the layer's real bias is absorbed into
  a downstream BatchNorm that is not folded into this datapath).
- **No downstream U-Net layers.** This is not encoder/decoder inference,
  not a full U-Net, and not flood-segmentation inference, and no full
  model pipeline was assembled or evaluated.
- **No board testing.** All results are Vivado out-of-context synthesis
  results only; a LUT or DSP utilization figure above 100% (as seen on
  the 35T here) means implementation would fail, but this was not
  attempted -- only `synth_design` (netlist generation) was run.
- **No measured speedup.** No throughput or latency was measured on
  hardware; the 4-cycle pipeline latency is a simulated/synthesized
  cycle-accurate property, not a measured one.
- **No measured board power.** All power figures are Vivado's
  vector-less, "Medium confidence" `report_power` estimates, not
  measurements from a running device.
- **Vivado power is a tool estimate**, for both the 200T and the optional
  35T comparison run.
- **200T synthesis is a target comparison, not physical deployment.** The
  `xc7a200tsbg484-1` part was chosen as a larger, available Vivado
  synthesis target (see `larger_fpga_target_comparison_summary.md` for
  the selection rationale); no board of this or any part is claimed to be
  owned, tested, or deployed to.
- The requested-DSP-multiply figures reported by Vivado's overutilization
  warning (266, 529, 788 for 8/16/24 outputs) are larger than the raw
  architectural multiply count (216, 432, 648 respectively); the exact
  cause of this discrepancy was not investigated further and should not
  be assumed to be a simple 1:1 count of `w0..w8 x pixel` multiplies.

## Safe claims made in this document

- "The 24-output DSP-aware first-layer design passes GHDL on the toy
  input" (216/216 checks, Section 5).
- "The 24-output DSP-aware design synthesizes on Artix-7 200T" (meets
  timing at +2.760 ns, though with 1 DSP-overutilization warning and
  98.78% DSP utilization -- Section 7).
- "This provides a third real synthesized scaling point after 8 and 16
  outputs" (Section 9).
- "The result helps test whether the larger target makes more parallel
  first-layer DSP-aware scaling feasible" (Section 10) -- with the answer
  that feasibility held cleanly through 16 outputs but is no longer clean
  at 24 outputs on the 200T, and clearly fails (>100% LUT and DSP
  utilization) on the 35T.

This document does not claim that all 32 first-layer channels fit, that
the full model pipeline fits, that this is board-tested, or that this
proves measured speedup, measured power, or deployment readiness.

## 12. Recommended next step

Given that the 200T's DSP headroom is nearly exhausted at 24 outputs
(731/740, 98.78%) and the linear request trend (266, 529, 788) points to
a 32-output DSP-only mapping requiring roughly 1050+ DSPs against a
740-DSP budget, the most informative next experiment is **not** simply
building a 32-output DSP-aware design and expecting it to fit -- it should
be expected to overutilize DSPs on the 200T. Two more productive next
steps, in order of priority:

1. Examine the existing 24-output 200T checkpoint
   (`stream_conv3x3_3chan_24out_cell_dsp_synth.dcp`) to characterize
   exactly which multiplies fell back to LUT fabric, to understand
   whether Vivado's automatic DSP+LUT split is a reasonable one or could
   be improved (e.g., via explicit `use_dsp` tuning) before committing to
   a full 32-output run.
2. If a 32-output design is still built, treat it explicitly as a
   "DSP+LUT mixed mapping" experiment rather than a "does it fit cleanly"
   experiment -- report the resulting LUT/DSP split and whether the
   design still meets timing under LUT fallback, rather than expecting a
   0-warning result like the 8- and 16-output designs.
